## 2. Legend or unverified: what to caveat

- **The German "spy".**
  - The Smithsonian's own record describes catching "a German soldier" near the trenches. It does not use the word spy.
  - Connecticut History and The World War's summary of the Smithsonian resource say "spy".
  - The Commons description says only that he is "said" to have helped capture a German soldier, and adds that his exploits' "true nature… will likely never be known".
  - Treat the capture as a legend.
- **Official sergeant rank.**
  - The Smithsonian record I opened never says he was made a sergeant. It calls him "mascot".
  - Britannica calls him "unofficial mascot". Britannica and Connecticut History say the promotion came for the spy capture.
  - Wikipedia lists "Sergeant" but also calls him "unofficial mascot".
  - I found no army document. Treat the rank as a legend or honorific.
- **Medals.**
  - Verified: the Smithsonian says a Humane Society medal was presented by General Pershing, and that he was given American Legion and YMCA memberships. The Smithsonian text says "Humane Society". Other sources say Humane Education Society, so keep it generic.
  - Not verified: the "most decorated dog of WWI" claim and the "nine medals" count (War History Online). I found no primary source for either.
  - Also unverified: an Iron Cross taken from the spy.
- Keep the whole caveat to one short line.
