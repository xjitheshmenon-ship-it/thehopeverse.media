### Performance in flight
- **Right lever:** NASA SP-4201 says he pushed the continuous-avoidance lever about 50 times and took only 2 shocks for bad timing.
- **Left lever:** His score on the discrete-avoidance lever was perfect.
- **Reaction time:** It averaged 0.82 s on the blue-light lever in flight, against 0.80 s in the preflight tests. This is the best number for the Short. Source: NASA SP-4201. California Science Center and NASA LSDA confirm the same conclusion in words.
- **The two shocks:** According to Wikipedia (citing Rohles 1963), one came from a fault in the test apparatus and the other from a lapse after the 14 g reentry. This is single-source.
- **Human comparison:** I found no astronaut reaction-time figure in what I opened. The usable comparison is Ham in space against Ham on the ground, 0.82 s against 0.80 s. Do not invent an astronaut number. Wikipedia only says chimps were chosen partly because their response time is close to humans'.

## Precision on "first"
- **Correct:** Ham was the first ape or hominid launched into space. Wikipedia describes him as the first great ape in space and calls Gagarin the "second hominid".
- **Correct:** Gagarin flew on 12 April 1961, and Shepard on 5 May 1961 aboard Freedom 7.
- **Correct:** Ham flew about 11 weeks before Gagarin and 14 weeks before Shepard.
- **Wrong or misleading:** "Before the American astronauts" is true, but "first in space" is not. Per Wikipedia, Albert II (a rhesus monkey) was the first mammal in space in 1949, and Laika orbited in 1957. Soviet dogs flew first.
- **Wording to use:** "first great ape in space", or "first chimpanzee in space".
- **Not the first chimp in orbit:** Enos was the only chimp to orbit, on 29 Nov 1961. Wikipedia lists this.
- **Wrong in your brief:** "Ham was the first American" is not a claim to make.
