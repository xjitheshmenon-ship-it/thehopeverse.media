## Open-source status (the most commonly misreported point)
- **The software is open source.** That covers the SDK, the robot software and a separate Microduck RL repo.
- **The hardware is not open.** Pollen's press kit says the mechanical and electronic designs are closed. It asks reporters not to call the robot "open-source hardware", and a representative says there are no plans to open it (TNW, BigGo).
- Pollen's own page does say "open source". A safe line is "open-source software on a closed-hardware robot".

## Ship dates (changed)
- **Launch promise:** first deliveries before Christmas 2026 in North America, Europe and the UK (Pollen blog, 27 Aug).
- **Latest (24 Oct):** the store page estimates 4–6 months for new orders and no longer promises Christmas (Gadgeteer).
- Some outlets, such as Hyper.ai and Mezha, still repeat "ships for Christmas". That is stale for new buyers.
- Early orders might still arrive before Christmas, but I found no confirmation.

## Unconfirmed or conflicting
1. **The 20,000 figure** is a Delangue forecast. It is unclear whether it counts paid deposits or refundable reservations, and I didn't find the refund terms.
2. **"Most-sold robot of 2026"** is Delangue's own "probably" and is unverified. Don't claim it as a record.
3. **Nvidia buying Hugging Face.** TNW (31 Aug) said the deal was only reported, at about $12.9bn, with no confirmation. A Gate.com piece says it closed in September. I couldn't verify that in primary sources, so leave it out or say "reported".
4. **Tariffs and VAT.** The $399 is before tax and shipping, so the EU and US landed price will be higher. I found no figure.
5. **LiDAR or depth sensor:** the sources disagree (see above).
6. **Safety and privacy.** There is a camera, a microphone and a camera-use indicator light. Pollen expects it to be used in homes (TNW).

## Why it matters for home and hobby robotics
- At $399 it sits in the price range of a games console, not a lab robot. You can use the gamepad with no code, or train your own behaviors.
- Its closest comparison is Reachy Mini, which Pollen says sold more than 10,000 units.
- Pollen and Hugging Face frame it as a development platform for ages 16 and up, not a toy, and as an educational testbed rather than a household helper (Gadgeteer, BigGo).
- It follows the open-model playbook: Delangue pitches open robots against "a black box system" (TNW).
