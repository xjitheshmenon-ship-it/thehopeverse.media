## 2. The method

- **Boxes:** two clear plastic boxes, each marked with a team's flag. EN says each held a mussel or an oyster. DE says mussel (Miesmuschel) only. For the Short, say "des moules" or "de la nourriture".
- **The pick:** the box he opened first counted as the predicted winner.
- **No draws:** EN notes there was no way to pick a draw, even though group matches can end in one.
- **Source gap:** the "box opened first" rule is in EN only. I did not check it against DE.

## 3. Origin, death and age

- **Hatched:** 26 January 2008 at Sea Life Weymouth, England, then moved to Sea Life Oberhausen (EN). DE says "im Atlantik bei Weymouth".
- **Died:** found dead on the morning of 26 October 2010, after staff saw him in good health the day before (EN). DE says he died of natural causes overnight.
- **Age caveat:** EN's infobox says "aged 2" and its text says "two-and-a-half". The dates give about 2 years 9 months. Use "moins de 3 ans" or "2 ans et 9 mois".
- **Warning, same octopus?** EN notes doubts that "Paul" was the same octopus in 2008 and 2010. The dates make this worth noting, because he would have been about 4 to 5 months old at Euro 2008. Avoid saying "il a prédit l'Euro 2008" as a settled fact.

## 4. Odds and explanations

- **Odds:** 8 correct coin-flip picks is (1/2)^8 = 1 in 256. This is my own arithmetic and assumes no draws. The sources say 1 in 64 for six in a row (see below).
- **Experts:** per EN, Professors Chris Budd (Bath) and David Spiegelhalter (Cambridge) compared Paul's run to coin-tossing. Spiegelhalter's point is that other animals tried and failed, and only the successful ones get attention afterwards. This is survivorship bias.
- **Flag preference (DE):** Shelagh Malham (Bangor University) said octopuses distinguish brightness and are drawn to pronounced horizontal shapes. So Paul may have favoured the German, Spanish and Serbian flags. Spain has a stronger horizontal stripe, and Serbia is brighter. This is presented as a hypothesis.
- **Colour vision:** EN says *Octopus vulgaris* is "almost certainly" colour blind.
- **Smell and taste (DE):** a scientist is quoted saying slight scent differences or a slightly bigger mussel could decide the choice. Octopus arms have taste receptors.
- **Name gap:** the DE page names only some of the scientists. I did not capture the Greifswald scientist's name.

## 5. Aftermath

- **Spain's government:** after the Germany–Spain semi-final, Prime Minister Zapatero offered Paul state protection (EN). DE words it as him being "worried". Industry Minister Miguel Sebastián called for Paul to get safe haven in Spain, after German fans called for Paul to be eaten (EN).
- **Offers:** DE reports that Carballiño (Ourense) raised €30,000 to bring Paul to its octopus festival. The Zoo Aquarium de Madrid negotiated an exchange (DE, citing DPA, Diario de Sevilla and the Guardian).
- **Response:** Sea Life rejected the offers, and the Carballiño mayor's group said Paul would not be on the menu.
- **Press:** more than 200 journalists were at Oberhausen before the final, including Spanish TV (DE).
- **Death:** a condolence book was opened and Paul was cremated. A monument was unveiled on 20 January 2011: a football topped by an octopus figure, with an urn holding his ashes (DE).
- **Successor:** Paul II, from Montpellier, took over in November 2010 (DE).
- **Criticism:** Iran's president Ahmadinejad called him a symbol of Western decadence (EN).
- **Rivals:** Mani the parakeet of Singapore picked the Netherlands in the final and was wrong (EN).
- **Documentary:** *The Life and Times of Paul the Psychic Octopus*, released in 2012.
