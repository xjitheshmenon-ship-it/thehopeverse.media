## Facts that need careful wording

**Height.**
- Wikipedia estimates 3.23 m (10 ft 7 in) at the shoulder, against Barnum's claim of about 4 m (13 ft 1 in).
- Wikidata says 3.5 m, so the sources disagree.
- Safe line: "environ 3,2 m selon les estimations; Barnum prétendait près de 4 m."
- Don't call him "the biggest elephant in the world" as a fact. That was Barnum's marketing.

**Origin.** Your brief says Sudan/Eritrea around 1861–62. The sources are looser than that.
- The Shoshani, Shoshani and Dahlinger paper says "exact place of origin is unknown."
- A blog quoting the Abyssinia/Ethiopia version appeared in one result. It is weak.
- etymology.life says Sudan around 1860. It is also weak.
- Safe line: "capturé tout jeune en Afrique de l'Est, région du Soudan/Abyssinie, vers 1860-61."
- Don't say Eritrea.

**Etymology of "jumbo".**
- Honest wording: "l'origine du nom est inconnue."
- The Shoshani paper says the origin of the name is unknown, and that it most likely came from Angola ("onjamba" = elephant).
- Wiktionary only says to compare Swahili *jambo* ("matter, thing") and *jumbe* ("chief").
- Popular sites state Swahili as fact. Treat that as unproven.
- Wiktionary also says the elephant gave the word its general meaning. The pre-1882 existence of "jumbo" as a word is **unverified**, because I found no source. Don't claim it either way.

**Queen Victoria.** There are two separate claims, and the second is shaky.
- Rides for her children: Wikipedia says Jumbo gave rides to children, "including those of Queen Victoria." Several sites repeat it, but they all look derived from the same source, so it is **weakly supported**.
- "100,000 schoolchildren wrote to the Queen": Wikipedia says 100,000 school children wrote to Queen Victoria, and many sites repeat it. I found no primary source, and Wikipedia itself adds a footnote to the number.
- Safe line: "On raconte que 100 000 écoliers auraient écrit à la reine." Never state it as fact. Don't say the Queen intervened. One site claims "the Queen's objections", and I found nothing else to back that.

**Refusal to enter the crate.**
- Documented: on 20 Feb 1882 Scott persuaded Jumbo to walk through his travelling crate outside the Elephant House. That is from the CBC photo caption.
- The popular story, in which Jumbo lay down in the street and refused to move, is **unverified in my sources**.
- Safe line: "Il aurait refusé de monter dans sa caisse; seul Scott réussit à le convaincre." Say "selon la légende" for the lying-down version. Scott's own book is the likely source.

**The 2017 bone study.**
- Your brief names "Nyamba/Holly Miller." I found no "Nyamba." The team I found was:
  - Holly Miller, who handled the isotope work.
  - Richard Thomas, the paleopathologist.
  - John Hutchinson.
  - They worked on the documentary *Jumbo: The Life of an Elephant Superstar*.
- It is a **documentary-based investigation. I did not find a peer-reviewed paper.** Say "des chercheurs" or "une équipe de scientifiques", not "a 2017 study."
- Findings, from the CBC:
  - Jumbo died at 24, yet his bones and joints looked like those of an elephant of 60.
  - His soft captive diet meant his teeth did not wear down.
  - John Hutchinson's blog adds that the growth plates were not all fused, which fits a male elephant still growing in his 20s.
- Don't say "stress from work" as a proven cause. The sources say bad diet and management or health problems.
- **Strongest unique fact:** a 24-year-old whose bones looked 60. It has two sources.

**The Tom Thumb legend.**
- Wikipedia says Barnum told the "possibly fictional" story that Jumbo saved a young elephant, Tom Thumb, and died himself in the process.
- The Simple Wikipedia page captions the illustration as "Barnum's tall tale."
- Safe line: "Barnum raconta qu'il avait sauvé un bébé éléphant — une histoire probablement inventée."
- Don't call it proven false. Say "probablement."

**The warm friendship angle.**
- The CBC says Jumbo was comforted by his long-time friend and keeper, Matthew Scott.
- elephant.se says Scott never married and lived with Jumbo for his whole life. It also mentions "sharing bottles of whiskey," so skip that detail for older audiences.
- The CBC book interview offers a counterpoint: Scott had become "a law unto himself" and was the only one who could manage Jumbo.
- "Scott's claim Jumbo responded to him" is plausible from the crate episode. I have no direct quote, so phrase it as "Scott était le seul à pouvoir le guider."
