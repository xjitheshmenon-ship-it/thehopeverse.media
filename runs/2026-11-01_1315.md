## Honest caveats
1. **It shows discrimination, not understanding.** The cats noticed a change. That doesn't prove they know the name means "me".
2. **Fixed order.** The own name was always the fifth sound. A counterbalanced design would be stronger. The authors did check the acoustics and found no clear difference between the nouns and the names.
3. **Many cats didn't habituate.** In Exp 2, only 6 of 24 household cats did. That is a tiny base for the finding that household cats pick out their own name from other cats' names.
4. **The café result is one café with 9–10 cats.** The authors say it can't be generalised.
5. **The reaction was small.** It was ear and head turns, not coming when called.
6. **It was in Japan, in Japanese, with mostly neutered indoor cats.**

## Later study: Takagi et al. 2022
Takagi, Saito, Arahori et al., "Cats learn the names of their friend cats in their daily lives", *Scientific Reports* 12:6155 (April 2022; I did not verify the exact day). https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-10261-5

- **Method:** cats heard a name called by their owner, then saw a photo of a face on a laptop. If the face didn't match the name, household cats looked at the screen longer. That suggests surprise.
- **Café cats:** they did not show this effect, which echoes 2019.
- **Cats tested:** Exp 1 used 48 cats, 29 from five cat cafés and the rest in homes. Exp 2 repeated it with human family members' names and faces. There, cats from larger families looked longer at mismatches.
- **How they learn:** the authors say cats probably learn by watching third-party interactions. They think direct rewards are "highly unlikely" to be the route. That partly contrasts with the 2019 reward explanation.
- **Limit:** the authors say they could not rule out an age effect in Exp 2.
- **Coverage:** Salon, 16 May 2022 (https://www.salon.com/2022/05/16/cats-know-the-names-of-other-cats-that-are-their-friends-study-says/); ScienceAlert, 16 May 2022.
