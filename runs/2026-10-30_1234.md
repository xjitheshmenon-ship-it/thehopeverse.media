## 1. Corrections to your brief
- **Dog kibble was never paired with pine nuts.** The "planning for breakfast" test used only pine nuts. The "breakfast choice" test used peanuts versus dog kibble.
- **The title "1 geai" is wrong.** The paper says "In the 'planning for breakfast' experiment, n = 8, in the 'breakfast choice' experiment, n = 9."
- **No jay is named.** The paper gives sex only: five females and three males, all adults.

## 2. Exact design
Source: Raby, Alexis, Dickinson & Clayton, *Nature* 445:919–921, 22 Feb 2007. Link: https://www.nature.com/articles/nature05575. Full text: https://bec.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/108/archive/papers/Clayton_4.25.07a.pdf

**Planning for breakfast (8 birds)**
- **Housing:** Each jay had three adjoining compartments, A, B and C. A and C held identical caching trays. B held the evening food bowl.
- **Training:** Training ran 6 mornings, with A and C on alternate days.
  - Each morning the jay was shut in A or C for 2 hours.
  - In one compartment it got a breakfast of powdered pine nuts. In the other it got nothing.
  - The powder could be eaten but not cached, so the birds could not cache during training.
  - Each bird had 3 "breakfast" and 3 "no-breakfast" mornings.
- **Evening routine:** The evening routine stayed the same throughout. Food was removed for 90 minutes. Then the jay got powdered pine nuts in compartment B for 30 minutes.
- **Test:** On one evening, the powder was replaced by 30 whole, cacheable pine nuts. The trays were in A and C.
- **Result:** The birds cached more in the "no-breakfast" compartment.
  - The means were 16.3 ± 1.8 pine nuts in the no-breakfast compartment versus 5.4 ± 1.8 in the breakfast compartment (mean ± s.e.m.).
  - The statistics were t(7) = 3.01, P = 0.02.
  - These are group means per bird, not totals.

**Breakfast choice (9 birds)**
- **Birds:** Two jays were added and one was excluded, leaving 9.
- **Training:** One compartment always gave dog kibble at breakfast and the other always gave peanuts. Both foods were given in powdered form.
- **Test:** In the evening the birds got 15 whole peanuts and 15 whole kibble pieces.
- **Result:** They cached more of the food that differed from that compartment's breakfast.
  - F(1,8) = 5.48, P = 0.047.
  - The paper's Fig. 2 gives no exact means in the text, so I have no per-compartment counts.
- **Why it matters:** This test rules out a "hunger associated with a place" explanation. Both compartments gave breakfast, so only the food type differed.

**Details most coverage misses**
- It was a single test trial per bird, after 6 training trials.
- The birds were not allowed to recover their caches the next morning.
- The tests were run one bird at a time per pair.

## 3. Human comparison
- **The paper's own claim:** The authors write that children develop a sense of the future at around age 2, "and some planning ability by only the age of four to five." They cite Atance & Meltzoff 2005 and Suddendorf & Busby 2005.
- **Safest wording:** "avant 4–5 ans" (before 4–5 years). The "under about 4" in your brief is slightly too young.
- **Suddendorf et al. 2011:** A review in PMC describes it this way: "4 but not 3 year olds" chose the correct item. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7145970/
- **Russell, Alexis & Clayton 2010:** As summarised by Warneken & Young, only 5-year-olds succeeded in a task where children picked two objects for another round of a game. https://www.eva.mpg.de/documents/Elsevier/Warneken_Young_CogDev_2014_2032482.pdf
- **A link between the studies:** D. M. Alexis is a co-author of both the jay paper and the children's study. I saw this in the citation lists but did not check it further.

## 4. Honest caveats
- **The authors' own limit:** They write that "in the absence of language, there is no knowing" whether this is episodic future thinking (the bird imagining itself in tomorrow morning) or semantic future thinking (acting for the future without mental time travel). Either way, they say the jays can plan "at least into tomorrow."
- **Suddendorf & Corballis 2007:** *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 30(3):299–313. Their abstract cautions that such data "do not provide convincing evidence" for mental time travel. https://philpapers.org/rec/SUDMTT-2
- **Their follow-up:** Their 2008 *Animal Behaviour* piece, "New evidence for animal foresight?", is summarised on Academia as arguing that jay caching could reflect past experience rather than anticipation. I saw only that summary, not the paper. https://www.academia.edu/17443950/New_evidence_for_animal_foresight
- **Eurasian jays, Cheke & Clayton 2012:** *Biology Letters* 8:171–175.
  - The title says Eurasian jays overcome current desires to plan for two distinct future needs.
  - All 13 birds developed specific satiety, which is a falling appetite for one food that was just eaten.
  - I could not open the planning-experiment numbers, so I would not quote them.
  - https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rsbl/article-abstract/8/2/171/49083/ and https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1209926110
- **Replication:** A 2020 *Scientific Reports* paper, "Testing two competing hypotheses for Eurasian jays' caching for the future," re-tests this work. I could only see its intro and could not open the results, so I cannot say whether it replicates. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-80515-7
- **Small samples:** n = 8 and n = 9 with P = 0.02 and 0.047 are modest. The Short should say "dans cette étude" (in this study), not "prouve" (proves).
