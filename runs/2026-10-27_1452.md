## What the search confirmed
- **Jelbert et al. 2014, PLOS ONE (published 26 March 2014).** The press release says New Caledonian crows may understand how to displace water to receive a reward, with the causal understanding level of a 5-7 year-old child. The authors describe the crows' understanding as sophisticated—but incomplete. Sources: [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2014-03-crows-basic-aesop-fable-task.html), [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/03/140326182039.htm), [paper PDF](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0092895&type=printable).
- **Logan et al. 2014, PLOS ONE (23 July 2014).** This is a separate study. The headline from [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/07/140723180824.htm) is that crows performed as well as 7- to 10-year-olds on some water-displacement tasks. The tasks were modified, and the paper is [here](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0103049). That is where the "7–10" figure comes from.
- **Logan et al. 2016, PLOS ONE (the "object-bias" study).** This one tempers the claim. It tested crows and children aged 5–10. After training to prefer the non-functional object, crows, but not children, performed more poorly on these two object-choice tasks. [Paper](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0168056).
- **Bird and children comparison.** The comparison is also much less clear than the press releases suggest. A 5–7 figure appears in the 2014 release, and a 7–10 figure appears in the July 2014 one.

## Unverified details (from memory)
- **Weir, Chappell & Kacelnik 2002, *Science* 297:981.** "Betty" bent a straight piece of wire into a hook to lift a small bucket of food out of a vertical tube. Her partner Abel had taken the hooked wire, leaving Betty only straight wire. She made a hook in about 9 of 10 trials. **[memoire; verify the count]** Betty had not been trained to do this. She was also reported to have bent wire in other ways, and later work showed that wild crows are very skilled at making hooks.
- **Natural tools.** Wild crows make hooked tools from twigs and stepped (cut) tools from pandanus leaves. The best-known sources are Hunt 1996 (*Nature*) and Hunt & Gray 2003. **[memoire]** These tools are used to extract insect larvae from wood. Different regions of New Caledonia have different pandanus tool designs, which suggests cultural transmission. **[memoire; Hunt & Gray 2003, Proc R Soc B]**
- **Bayern et al. 2018, *Scientific Reports* 8:15676, "Compound tool construction by New Caledonian crows."** Crows combined parts to make a longer tool. About 4 of 8 crows did so, and one crow built it without being shown how. **[memoire; verify numbers]** Some of the crows were "naive" about this particular task, though not about tools in general.
- **Jelbert 2014 sample.** The experiments used 6 wild-caught crows (temporarily captive, on Grande Terre). The crows chose heavier and sinking objects, and chose water-filled tubes over sand-filled ones. **[memoire; verify]**

## The title: where it overclaims
- **"Intelligence d'un enfant de 7 ans" is not a supported statement.** The studies compared crows with children on **one narrow causal task**, not on overall intelligence. The 5–7 and 7–10 figures also don't agree.
- **Crows are not the same as children.** The 2016 study found that crows relied on object biases and children did not. It suggests the crows' success may partly reflect a different mechanism from the children's.
- **"Fabrique des outils" is accurate.** It is well supported by the field and lab studies.
- **A small sample limits the claims.** The captive experiments used a small number of birds.

## Wording suggestions (French)
- **Safe title:** « Ce corbeau fabrique des outils… et résout un casse-tête d'enfant de 5 à 7 ans » (or « comme un jeune enfant dans un test précis »).
- **Alternative:** « Corbeau de Nouvelle-Calédonie : un génie des outils qui rivalise avec de jeunes enfants… sur un test précis. »
- **Avoid:** « aussi intelligent qu'un enfant de 7 ans ».
- **In the script:** say "dans un test de déplacement d'eau, certains corbeaux ont réussi comme des enfants de 5 à 7 ans" and "les chercheurs parlent d'une compréhension sophistiquée mais incomplète."
