# Gaganyaan CM–SM separation tests: research brief

I ran one search and three fetches. I couldn't open ISRO's own page (the fetch was blocked), so every ISRO statement below comes second-hand through press reports. Check the original ISRO post on X or isro.gov.in before the script is queued. I did not check YouTube trending, so I can't say which high-viewing topic this connects to.

## 1. What was tested

- **Announcement and place:** On 16 October 2026 ISRO announced it had completed a series of Crew Module (CM)–Service Module (SM) separation tests at VSSC, Thiruvananthapuram. (sourced, Organiser, 20 Oct 2026)
- **Date caveat:** GKToday says the tests ran on 16 and 17 October. Say "announced 16 Oct" or "mid-October", not "completed 16 Oct". (sourced)
- **Setup:** ISRO built a heavily instrumented test setup at VSSC that simulated the integrated CM–SM assembly. (sourced, SSBCrack, 16 Oct)
- **What happened:** The engineers staged the separation, including cutting several of the umbilical lines (power and fluid cables) between the modules. ISRO's verdict, as The Week reports it, was that the separation system was properly designed. It also said the capsule's internal systems absorbed the separation shock. (sourced, The Week, 17 Oct)
- **Systems that survived:** The Week lists these:
  - avionics
  - the inertial navigation system
  - the thermal protection system (the heat shields)
  - the umbilical shield
  - the uprighting system
  - the thermal and humidity control system

  The last two depend on pyrotechnic devices. (sourced, The Week)
- **Context:** Earlier in 2026, ISRO qualified the uprighting system, the umbilical connect/disconnect mechanisms and the apex-cover separation loads. That is the context given by SSBCrackExams.
- **Scope:** This was a ground test. Nothing flew.

## 2. Why separation matters before re-entry

- **The sequence:** The two modules fly as one body from liftoff through the orbit phase. Before re-entry the Crew Module must leave the Service Module, which is then discarded. (sourced, The Week)
- **How it works:** Small pyrotechnic (explosive) charges sever the connections. Jettison systems then push the two modules apart. (sourced, The Week)
- **Why it's risky:** If a cable fails to cut, or the separation shock damages something inside the capsule, the return is endangered. This is an analyst's view. The Week attributes it to Girish Linganna, not to ISRO.
- **The point for viewers:** The Service Module keeps the capsule alive in orbit, and it has to be let go before the capsule can come home.

## 3. G1 status, using ISRO's own statements only

- **Official wording:** ISRO says the first uncrewed mission, G1, is targeted for launch in 2026. (sourced via Organiser, 20 Oct)
- **Chairman:** The Week (17 Oct) reports that V. Narayanan said in June the launch was expected in Q4 2026, and repeated in September that it would fly within the year. The Week also says "No date has been announced". (sourced)
- **Government:** PIB, 21 Jan 2026, said the uncrewed mission was likely in the second half of 2026. I only saw this through Wikipedia's citation, not on PIB itself. (sourced, secondary)
- **Slips:** G1 was first planned for December 2020, and as recently as last October it was expected in December 2025. (sourced, The Week)
- **Crewed flight:** The Week says 2027 is "targeted", but I found no ISRO statement for it. Don't state a crewed date. Say "after the uncrewed flights".
- **Unverified:** Some reports point to a launch window between mid-October and early November. It is unconfirmed, and nothing had launched by 25 Oct. Don't use it.
- **Don't copy:** The Week calls the first crewed mission "Gaganyaan-4". That naming doesn't match other sources.
