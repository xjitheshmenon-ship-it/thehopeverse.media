## Confirmed facts

**Study.** Newport, Wallis, Reshitnyk and Siebeck, "Discrimination of human faces by archerfish (*Toxotes chatareus*)", *Scientific Reports* 6:27523, published 07 June 2016, open access. [nature.com/articles/srep27523](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep27523). The work was done at the University of Oxford and the University of Queensland.

**Method.**
- A computer monitor showing images of human faces was positioned above the aquariums. The fish were trained to spit at a particular face. [phys.org (Oxford release)](https://phys.org/news/2016-06-fish-human.html)
- It was a two-alternative forced choice. The fish were trained to choose one face for a reward and to ignore a second, unrewarded face. [UQ](https://biomedical-sciences.uq.edu.au/article/2016/08/dr-ulrike-siebeck-research-finds-fish-can-distinguish-human-faces)
- Why spitting works as an answer: "There is no ambiguity in where they are shooting," said Newport. [CNN, 7 Jun 2016](https://www.cnn.com/2016/06/07/health/fish-human-face-recognition-study-trnd)
- Experiment 1 used 44 colour images of full faces.
- Experiment 2 used 18 greyscale images standardized for brightness, with an oval mask to remove head shape as a cue.

**Number of fish.**
- Experiment 1 used four fish. The paper says "we tested whether four archerfish could be trained". They were numbered fish (Fish 5, Fish 6, Fish 7…), so no fish has a published name.
- Training took 8–17 sessions.
- ⚠ I did not retrieve the number of fish in Experiment 2.

**Accuracy.**
- With 44 unfamiliar faces in the lineup, the fish spat at the correct face 81 percent of the time on average. With standardized features and black-and-white images, accuracy was about 86 percent. [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/54995-fish-can-recognize-human-faces.html)
- UQ gives 81% to 86% over the two experiments.
- The 86% figure is a higher score on a harder-controlled test. Don't say the fish got worse or better "because" of the removals. Say "even with colour and head shape removed".
- ⚠ Scientific American cites a range of 77–89% across individual fish. That is a single source and is not confirmed by the paper.
- ⚠ Chance level for the 44-face lineup was not retrieved. Check it before quoting "x times better than chance".

**Why it matters.**
- The paper says fish do not appear to possess neocortex-like cells and are unlikely to have evolved specialised capabilities for human face recognition.
- Previous animals that passed this kind of task all have a neocortex or neocortex-like structures. Most are also domesticated.
- Newport said distinguishing many human faces is hard because all faces share the same basic features. [Sci.News](https://www.sci.news/biology/fish-recognize-human-faces-03928.html)
- Human comparison: in humans, the fusiform gyrus in the neocortex is heavily involved in face processing. [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4895153/)
- ⚠ I found no head-to-head human accuracy figure on this task. Use the contrast in brain structure, not a human score.

## Later work and honest caveats
- **Newport 2018** (*Animal Behaviour* 145:39–49). The fish were tested with the learned faces rotated about the vertical axis by 30°, 60° and 90°. [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0003347218302720)
  - The rotation at which fish could still recognise the face varied between individuals and experiments, from 30° to 90°.
  - Six fish were used. The faces were 3D-scanned renderings of two Caucasian women. Speed and accuracy decreased as rotation increased, and two fish could not recognise their trained face at some angles. This is from a citing paper's summary, so it is secondhand.
  - Newport said the slower, less accurate performance at higher rotation resembles what is seen in humans and other primates. [National Geographic, 2018](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/animals/article/archerfish-animal-cognition-intelligence-human-faces-news)
- **Limits.**
  - The sample is small: four fish in Experiment 1.
  - It is a trained, rewarded task using 2D images on a screen. It shows discrimination of images, not recognition of a person in real life.
  - Forbes (Jan 2026) notes the study doesn't definitively prove whether archerfish process faces with "templates" or piece by piece. [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/scotttravers/2026/01/17/meet-the-fish-that-can-recognize-human-faces---a-biologist-explains/)
  - A 2024 review says the 2018 results could also be explained by fish having mental representations of faces rather than view generalisation. So the mechanism is still open. [PMC11632839](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11632839/)
  - A 2022 paper summarises the work as fish discriminating a face "with apparent difficulty", as accuracy dropped markedly on rotated faces. This is a different framing from the 2018 press coverage. [PMC8918800](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8918800/)
