## 2. Monte Cassino crates: what is solid and what is not

- **Documented.** Many sources report that he carried ammunition, and the company badge shows it. HES says eyewitness accounts describe it as a common sight.
- **Thin evidence.** Wikipedia states that the story "generated debate over its accuracy". Its only named support is "at least one account" from a British soldier.
- **Interested witness.** [Forces News](https://www.forcesnews.com/feature/wojtek-bear-how-cigarette-eating-ammunition-carrying-bear-helped-boost-morale-polish) quotes Allan Pollock, who wrote a children's book on Wojtek, saying there are "multiple witness statements". It also describes the whole story as sitting between truth and myth. I did not see the statements themselves.
- **Crate size.** Wikipedia says 45 kg (100 lb) crates of 25-pounder shells that "normally required four men". That is one source and the figures look inflated, so don't state them as fact. HES and BL say only "heavy shells/large boxes".
- **Not found.** I found no wartime document or photo of him carrying a crate. The badge itself is a symbol, not proof.
- **BL version.** He mimicked the men, stood upright with paws out and took boxes from lorries to the guns, even under fire. That is the legend as told by the community.

**Suggested honest wording:** « Selon des témoins, il portait des caisses d'obus. Les historiens débattent des détails, mais ses camarades en ont fait leur emblème. »
